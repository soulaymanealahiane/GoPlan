### BBA exact options and exceptions

Nine current concentrations: Finance; Management; Marketing; Logistics and Supply Chain Management; International Business; Analytics and AI for Business; Technology and Innovation Management; Sustainable Development and Social Enterprise; FinTech. Catalogue p123 and p126 list nine. BBA sheet p4 introduction says six, but that same applicable sheet explicitly adds TIM/SDE on p7 and FinTech on p8, noting Fall 2025 launch. Do not remove the newer three based on the stale introduction.

Catalogue p126 explicitly calls second concentration “another business discipline”; duplicate primary/secondary selection must not be possible. Sheet p4 limits special-topic courses to two per concentration. Management/HRD shared-course handling appears in the sheet pp4-5 and must not become automatic double credit.

BBA sheet p9 explicitly allows Leadership (16 SCH) under SBA; this specific current-sheet list is a meaningful exception to catalogue p123's general “minor outside SBA” wording. Do not exclude Leadership merely because it belongs to SBA. The p9 list also supplies CS, Mathematics, International Studies, HRD, Organizational Studies, Communication Studies, Gender Studies, African Studies, Psychology and English. A Business Administration minor is not a qualifying BBA secondary minor. The sheet delegates external-school minor requirements to the providing school. Sheet p14 gives 129-130 total, with certain 16-SCH minors raising total; total credit checks must reflect the chosen minor's actual credits.

The source says a minor is not required when selecting a second concentration; it does not say the university forbids a student from pursuing approved extra study. Enforcing two ordinary alternative pathways in GoPlan is correct; claiming an absolute university prohibition against any extra minor would overstate this evidence.

### EMS and GE exact options

EMS p185: Manufacturing and Logistics Engineering; Decision Support Systems; Biotechnology; Finance. It explicitly requires nine SCH from a thematic area. GE p189: Mechanical Engineering; Mechatronics; Manufacturing and Logistics Engineering; Biotechnology; Aeronautics; Computer Science. It explicitly requires 12 SCH from a thematic area. Other thematic areas require collaboration and approval from academic advisor and academic coordinator; EMS p186 also mentions Program Lead and planned educational leave. These are approval paths, not unconstrained free-text focus choices.

Both full degree component lists omit a minor because the standard degree uses thematic-area credits instead. The correct default interface omits minor and second thematic area, while identifying approved extra study as outside the supported standard pathway. General catalogue p120 discusses minors and individual minors; absence of a standard minor component is not a universal ban on any additional approved credential.

### CS second specialization, minors and overlap

Catalogue p178 explicitly disallows counting one course toward two objectives. When required specialization courses overlap, a suitable replacement from the eligible elective set must be approved. Overlapping elective sets require distinct chosen electives. Two distinct labels alone do not prove both specializations are satisfied. Catalogue p178 also allows the elective three SCH to consist of multiple one/two-SCH courses; do not force every specialization into exactly three physical courses.

CS p182 requires a minor outside Computing. Additional explicit minor restrictions: CS minor unavailable to computing-based programs (p194); Data Analytics minor unavailable to Computing and EMS programs (p195); General Engineering minor unavailable to EMS and GE (p196); Mechatronics minor unavailable to GE (p197). Mathematics requires credits beyond the major; CS Mathematics Individual Minor excludes required-major MTH courses and requires approval (p199, general Individual Minor validation p120). Approved custom slots remain proposals, not confirmed adviser-approved courses.

### Same-major minors and approval-only cases

BSESS's named Environmental Studies minor contains mandatory GEO1301/GEO2305 (pp157-158), both already required by BSESS major p147. There is no explicit sentence on pp157-158 saying BSESS students are banned. A standard same-major minor is unsupported and cannot be satisfied by recopying major courses; any alternate approved construction needs review. Describe the UI exclusion as standard-pathway/no-double-counting enforcement rather than quote an unsupported universal ban.

General Individual Minor p120 requires a coherent approved 15-SCH collection outside the major and school validation. Merely typing a label, acknowledging a disclaimer, or choosing an arbitrary course cannot confer approval. Catalogue p119 requires timely declaration by the semester of the 60th SCH and academic-adviser involvement; app selection is a planning proposal, not official declaration.

## Graduate terminology cross-check

MSSE, MSSEM and MSBDA supplied sheets distinguish Master Thesis and Master Project; those should be completion routes, not “focus” or “minor”. MBA distinguishes thesis and non-thesis tracks, the latter with four concentration courses or concentration plus internship. Older MACDM/MAISD sheets contain historical concentration and capstone details; current applicability needs checking before promoting them into current degree selectors. No bachelor-minor selector should be applied to graduate profiles. This audit prioritizes all eight supported bachelor programs; graduate prerequisite/course equivalence validation is not claimed complete.

## Structural acceptance criteria

1. One policy matrix should drive available UI fields, default recommendations, manual override validation, server-side updates, import normalization and saved roadmap review.
2. BBA second concentration/minor alternatives are mutually exclusive and duplicate concentrations rejected. CS second specialization keeps minor and consumes corresponding elective budget. EMS/GE minor fields must disappear; no empty More choices disclosure.
3. Programs without named tracks should show actual major requirement/course choices without an empty or fabricated focus selector.
4. Ineligible/stale values from switching degrees cannot survive silently in requests or restored profiles. Preserve existing student courses/progress until explicit reconstruction; flag historic unsupported combinations for review.
5. User-facing labels, reasons, course categories, guided-building choices, manual updater and exports should use the same program-specific terminology.
6. Verification must exercise forged/stale requests as well as visible dropdowns. No course may acquire a second counted requirement merely because a UI selection changed.
